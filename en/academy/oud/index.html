01 / 06 — Idea

What is oud?

Oud or agarwood refers to resinous wood from certain Aquilaria trees, used in perfumery.

Character
  1. 01Material
  2. 02Accord
  3. 03Name

02 / 06 — Contrast

Where it comes from

Resin can develop in the wood under particular conditions; origin and treatment affect the material.

THE ASSUMPTION

If it is an Arabian fragrance, it contains oud.

Reconsider the idea ↗

Origin or marketing style does not replace product information. Check its notes and the maker’s description.

03 / 06 — Concepts

What it may smell like

It can be described as woody, resinous, smoky or animalic depending on material and formulation.

01Material+

Natural oud: an aromatic material.

02Accord+

A composition can evoke an oud impression.

03Name+

The product name does not disclose the formula.

04 / 06 — Practice

Natural oud and accords

Many perfumes use oud-like accords; a listed oud note does not prove natural oud oil is present.

05 / 06 — Decision

Read the description

Look for specific note descriptions and do not assume all oud fragrances smell alike.

You see “oud” in the name. What can you conclude?

It necessarily contains a known amount of natural oud.↗

The name alone gives neither the origin nor percentage of the materials.

I need more information about what the name represents.↗

Correct. Distinguish the name, scent description and documented material information.

06 / 06 — Apply

Before you buy

Sample when possible; oud can feel very different between formulas.
